“Ihr Logo. Jeder Schluck. Den ganzen Sommer.”

Score 93 / 100 +34% WoW demand 46% margin Delivery window: by 14 Jul
1

Who

Target audience

HR managers and procurement leads at German Mittelstand companies (50–500 employees) sourcing employee gifts and event merchandise for Sommerfeste, team offsites, and onboarding before the summer break. Decision drivers: tight Q3 budgets, ESG reporting pressure, and a need for low-effort, branded items that staff actually keep.

2

What

Core message

A made-in-Europe stainless steel water bottle, laser-engraved or printed with the customer’s logo, delivered in tiered MOQ bundles (50 / 250 / 500+) with a guaranteed delivery window before mid-July. Positioned as the practical, reusable alternative to disposable summer-event swag.

3

Why

Value & offer

Reusable steel bottles cut single-use plastic at company events and contribute measurable data points to internal CSR and DSGVO-adjacent ESG reporting — without the vague claims that trigger LkSG and EmpCo scrutiny. For procurement, Tassendruck consolidates design, print, and EU-based fulfilment into one invoice.

4

How

Voice & visual

Voice is confident, plain-spoken Mittelstand German — concrete numbers (MOQ, lead time, recycled-steel content) instead of green adjectives. Visual angle: real bottles on real desks and at real Sommerfest tables, laser-engraving close-ups, daylight rather than studio gloss.

5

Where

Channels & usage

  • LinkedIn Ads — Sponsored Single Image + Lead Gen Form targeting HR / People Ops / Procurement at DACH companies 50–500 FTE.
  • Google Ads — German exact/phrase intent keywords; landing page with MOQ calculator and July delivery guarantee.
  • Tassendruck Blog (SEO) — pillar article ‘Nachhaltige Mitarbeitergeschenke 2026’ targeting mid-funnel research queries.
  • HubSpot Email — 3-email sequence to corporate prospect list: teaser → case snippet → deadline reminder.
  • SV Gruppe Internal Media — house ad in SV B2B newsletter; one regional portal slot.
  • Instagram / TikTok Organic — engraving line behind-the-scenes; customer Sommerfest unboxing; repurposed creative.

The thirteen points, simply

Every signal in one line — the whole engine input on a single card.

1 · the marketHow many people search this word per month, and in which season. It says nothing about us.
2 · where we standOur place for it — 1, 14, 40 — and which of our pages appears.
3 · the directionHow our place moved since the previous week’s photograph.
4 · the doorwaysHow close each word stands to the top 3 or to page one.
5 · the racesWho finishes ahead where we run — and the races we never entered at all.
6 · the rivals, measuredThe overall ranking of our real rivals — Tassendruck itself = 100.
7 · the searcher’s wishWhy the person is typing: to buy, to learn, to reach one site, or to visit a shop.
8 · the market’s money voteWhat one click would cost as an ad — expensive = proven commerce.
9 · the window displayWhich blocks Google shows for a word — we build what fits the window (SERP Features).
10 · the shop’s areasWhich part of the shop is climbing, and which is falling.
11 · the stop signWords where our own pages fight each other — fix first, promote later.
12 · clicks won or lost, estimatedEvery move priced in clicks — context until checked against real click data.
13 · the early warningRival pages that appeared on our words since the last photograph.

How to read this

Two honest limits, so the numbers are not over-read.

Relative, not absoluteThis source shows movement relative to a search’s own peak — never search volume. The absolute numbers come from the SEO source.
Thin curves are discardedSearches with too little measurable interest come back as mostly zeros, which can produce a convincing but false seasonal peak. Those are discarded rather than guessed.
